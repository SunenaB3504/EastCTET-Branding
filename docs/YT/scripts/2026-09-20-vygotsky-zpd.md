### 75–450 (2.5–15s) — Define ZPD once, cleanly
**On-screen:**
> **Zone of Proximal Development:** the gap between what a learner can do **alone**, and what they can do **with a more knowledgeable other**.

**VO:** `Zone of Proximal Development — ye woh gap hai jo bachcha akela nahi kar sakta, lekin kisi zyada knowledgeable insaan ki madad se kar leta hai. Na bahut aasaan, na bahut mushkil — bilkul beech mein.`

### 450–1050 (15–35s) — Animated diagram: 3 nested arcs
**On-screen (arcs label as VO reaches them):**
1. Inner arc: **"Can do alone"**
2. Middle arc (the ZPD): **"Can do with support"**
3. Outer arc: **"Cannot do yet, even with support"**

**VO:** `Sochiye teen circles hain. Sabse andar — jo bachcha akela kar sakta hai. Beech mein — ZPD, jahaan support se kar leta hai, aur yahin real learning hoti hai. Sabse bahar — jo abhi possible hi nahi, support se bhi nahi.`

**On-screen (scaffolding overlay, appears at ~28s):**
> **Scaffolding:** temporary support, gradually withdrawn as the learner grows more competent.

**VO:** `Ye support dene ka tareeka scaffolding kehlaata hai — temporary madad, jo competence badhne ke saath dheere dheere hataayi jaati hai. Aur ye support sirf teacher se nahi aata — capable peer bhi "more knowledgeable other" ho sakta hai.`

### 1050–1260 (35–42s) — How CTET asks it
**On-screen:**
> CTET's trap: a "scaffolding" option that's actually rote repetition. Real scaffolding is *responsive*, not repeated drilling.

**VO:** `CTET ek common galti test karta hai — rote rehearsal ko scaffolding bata dena. Asli scaffolding responsive hoti hai, sirf repeat karna nahi.`
