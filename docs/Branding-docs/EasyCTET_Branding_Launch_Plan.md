# EasyCTET — Branding & Launch Plan

*A ground-up plan starting from zero: domain, accounts, legal basics, brand identity, and promotion.*

## Current Season Brand Decision

This season, EasyCTET is positioned as a focused **CTET Paper 1 preparation app** covering:

- Child Development & Pedagogy (CDP)
- English
- Environmental Studies (EVS)
- Hindi
- Mathematics

Paper 2, Marathi, Science, and Social Studies are future roadmap areas, not launch promises. They should not appear in current taglines, store copy, campaign briefs, screenshots, or promotional posts.

## Founder & Business Context

EasyCTET is being built by a solo developer working full-time at an MNC, with a committed **6–8 hours per day outside the primary job**. There is no seed funding, dedicated team, or full-time marketing capacity. This is the founder's first production-grade app and a self-funded attempt to create additional revenue.

The first season is therefore a **focused validation launch** ahead of the 9 October exam. The available time is enough for a polished and properly tested Paper 1 release, but the business should still avoid premature scale, unnecessary features, and high-risk spending. The objective is to serve an initial group of real Paper 1 customers reliably, learn what they value, and validate willingness to pay. Full-scale growth, broader content coverage, and heavier promotion are planned for the next exam season after the product and business have evidence behind them.

### Season 1 Operating Principles

- Protect the founder's time: spend the available 6–8 hours on the highest-value product, quality, and customer work.
- Protect cash: use free or low-cost tools, avoid paid acquisition, and delay non-essential subscriptions and production assets.
- Protect trust: release fewer features, but make questions, explanations, payments, and support dependable.
- Learn from real users: treat feedback, activation, repeat practice, and paid conversions as the main outputs of this season.
- Keep the brand honest: present EasyCTET as focused and new, never as a large institution or an official CTET product.

### Season 1 Success Definition

Success is not a large download number. It is a stable first release, a small group of active Paper 1 learners, the first paying customers, useful feedback, and enough evidence to decide what deserves investment for next season.

## Branding Readiness Runbook

Use this sequence immediately before launch. A step is complete only when it has a real owner, a final asset, and a recorded link or decision.

### Step 1: Lock the identity

- Confirm the final name: **EasyCTET**.
- Use the approved tagline: **Serious CTET Paper 1 prep, simplified.**
- Use only the five current subjects: CDP, English, EVS, Hindi, and Mathematics.
- Confirm the primary domain and matching social handles.
- Keep the brand kit in one folder: logo, app icon, colors, fonts, tagline, short description, and contact email.

### Step 2: Lock the customer promise

- Write one final Play Store short description and one final website introduction.
- State what is included in the free tier and what Pro Pass unlocks.
- Remove every placeholder, unverified claim, and reference to future subjects.
- Keep the government non-affiliation statement visible where appropriate.
- Never promise qualification, a score, or a first-attempt result.

### Step 3: Make the website trustworthy

- Verify the homepage on a low-cost Android phone and a desktop browser.
- Test every navigation link, sample test, email link, and legal-page link.
- Replace placeholder Play Store links only after the app listing is live.
- Confirm the Privacy Policy and Terms contain the real operator name, support email, effective date, pricing, refund, and data practices.
- Check that the website loads without a build system, tracking scripts, or unnecessary paid services.

### Step 4: Prepare the launch kit

- Prepare one app icon, five clean screenshots, one feature graphic, and one short demo video.
- Prepare three launch messages: personal network, teacher community, and public social post.
- Prepare seven daily questions for the first week after launch.
- Prepare a simple support response for installation, billing, content, and technical issues.
- Create a small feedback form or dedicated support email before inviting users.

### Step 5: Soft launch and sign off

- Invite a small group of trusted Paper 1 learners first.
- Confirm installation, onboarding, free content, paid purchase flow, offline behavior, progress recovery, and support response.
- Record every serious issue and fix launch blockers before wider promotion.
- Publish broadly only after the app, website, and legal links tell the same story.

### Step 6: Review after launch

- Review feedback after 24 hours, 7 days, and the exam period.
- Track visits, launch-update requests, installs, first quiz completion, repeat practice, support issues, and paid conversions in a simple private spreadsheet.
- Decide next-season investment from observed demand rather than assumptions.

## Phase 0: Foundational Setup (Week 1)

These are prerequisites — do these before any branding/creative work, since your handles, domain, and legal name need to be locked and consistent everywhere.

### 1. Domain Registration
- [ ] Register **easyctet.in** (primary — .in signals Indian relevance for an India-specific exam, and is what you'd already planned)
- [ ] Also register **easyctet.com** if available — protects against squatters and future non-India expansion; redirect it to the .in site
- [ ] Consider **easyctet.app** as a backup option since it's an app-first product — not essential if .in and .com are secured
- [ ] Use a registrar with easy DNS management (GoDaddy India, Namecheap, or BigRock) — you'll need this for email (Phase 0.3) and later landing page hosting
- [ ] Enable domain privacy protection and auto-renewal

### 2. Handle/Account Registration — Reserve the Essentials First
Register the **exact same handle** everywhere to avoid brand confusion. Check availability first across all platforms before committing to a final spelling (e.g. "EasyCTET" vs "easy_ctet" vs "easyctetapp").

- [ ] Instagram: @easyctet (or @easyctet.app if taken)
- [ ] YouTube: /@easyctet — set up channel even before first video; claim the handle
- [ ] Telegram: t.me/easyctet — critical for this category, teacher-exam aspirants live on Telegram groups/channels
- [ ] WhatsApp Business/Channel: register business number early since WhatsApp Channels are high-reach for Indian exam prep audiences
- [ ] X (Twitter): @easyctet — reserve only if it takes negligible time
- [ ] Facebook Page: defer unless early users show that this is a useful channel
- [ ] LinkedIn Page: defer; it is not a Season 1 customer-acquisition priority

**Do this in one sitting** — a spreadsheet of handle, username, password (manager), status (claimed/pending) avoids losing track.

### 3. Business Basics (don't skip — these protect you and enable payments)
- [ ] Decide business structure: sole proprietorship is usually sufficient to start (simplest, since you're a solo developer); Udyam/MSME registration is free and quick if you want it
- [ ] Get a business email on your domain: hello@easyctet.in or support@easyctet.in (via Google Workspace or Zoho Mail — Zoho is free for 1 user on a custom domain, useful for a solo bootstrap)
- [ ] If you plan in-app payments for the freemium tier, decide payment gateway now (Razorpay/Google Play Billing) since this affects account KYC lead time — gateway KYC can take days, so start early
- [ ] Basic Privacy Policy and Terms of Service pages — required by Play Store before you can even submit the app; can draft these yourself or use a generator, but they must be hosted at a live URL (this is another reason the domain needs to be up early)

### 4. Play Store Developer Account
- [ ] Register Google Play Console developer account (one-time fee, ~$25) — do this now since Google's identity verification can take several days and blocks your launch timeline if left late

## Phase 1: Brand Identity (Weeks 1–2, parallel with Phase 0)

### Positioning
"A calm, trustworthy way to master CTET Paper 1" — the differentiator is **originally authored content** (not scraped past papers), with a clear explanation for every answer. Trust and rigor should be the core promise, not just "free."

### Final Seasonal Message

**Primary promise:** Understand the concept. Practice the pattern. Walk into CTET Paper 1 prepared.

**Supporting proof points:**
- Original questions across CDP, English, EVS, Hindi, and Mathematics.
- Explanations that teach the reasoning behind each answer.
- Focused practice designed for short study sessions and modest Android phones.
- Offline-friendly learning with calm progress tracking and no distracting hype.

**Primary audience:** CTET Paper 1 aspirants, especially D.El.Ed/B.Ed candidates, first-time applicants, and re-attempting educators studying in short sessions.

**Message pillars:**
1. **Understand, do not guess:** explain the why behind the answer.
2. **Practice what matters:** stay close to the Paper 1 pattern and syllabus.
3. **Study with dignity:** calm, clear, respectful preparation without fear-based selling.
4. **Keep moving offline:** useful practice on an everyday Android phone, even with unreliable connectivity.

**Recommended tagline:** `Serious CTET Paper 1 prep, simplified.`

**Campaign CTA:** `Start your Paper 1 practice.`

**Do not claim:** guaranteed qualification, official CTET affiliation, "crack CTET in days," complete Paper 2 preparation, or coverage of subjects not included this season.

### Naming Consideration
"Easy" in the name can undercut a "rigorous prep" positioning. You don't need to rename, but your tagline should counterbalance it:
- *"EasyCTET — Serious prep, simplified."*
- *"EasyCTET — Master the pattern, not just the past papers."*

### Visual Identity
- [ ] Logo: simple wordmark + icon (a book/pencil/checkmark motif is overused in this category — consider something distinctive like a compass or a graduation-cap-meets-checkmark)
- [ ] Color palette: avoid colors too close to Byju's (purple), Unacademy (green), Testbook (blue/orange) so you don't look derivative; consider teal + warm gold, or navy + saffron for an Indian-exam-context feel without being generic
- [ ] Typography: one clean sans-serif for UI (Poppins, Inter), one for headings if you want a distinct feel
- [ ] App icon: test at small sizes (Play Store thumbnail) early — many logos that look fine on a screen fail as a tiny icon
- [ ] Basic brand kit doc (colors, fonts, logo variants) — keeps your app UI, social posts, and website visually consistent from day one, important since you're a solo developer without a designer checking consistency for you

### Voice & Tone
Encouraging but exam-serious. Your audience is often re-attempting the exam and anxious about it — competence and empathy will land better than hype or gimmicks.

## Phase 2: Pre-Launch Content & Community Seeding (Weeks 2–6)

Goal: have an audience waiting on day one, not starting from zero downloads.

- [ ] Landing page on easyctet.in with an email/WhatsApp opt-in ("Notify me at launch" + early-bird referral code promise)
- [ ] Start with one or two sustainable posts per week on Instagram/YouTube Shorts: short CDP/EVS/Math concept explainers (Piaget, Kohlberg, environmental concepts, etc.) — consistency matters more than volume for a solo founder
- [ ] Launch the Telegram channel with a daily one-question drill — low effort, builds a habit loop, and gives you a distribution channel for launch day
- [ ] Contact a small number of relevant CTET-focused Telegram/YouTube channel admins or micro-influencers only after the product is stable; avoid paid deals during Season 1
- [ ] Draft Play Store listing assets in advance: screenshots, feature graphic, short/long description with ASO keywords ("CTET Paper 1", "CDP quiz", "CTET mock test", "CTET Paper 1 mock test", "English Hindi EVS Maths practice")

## Phase 3: Launch

- [ ] Submit to Play Store with a closed testing track first (Google requires this for new developer accounts before production release — factor this review time into your timeline)
- [ ] Launch with a simple, transparent early-customer offer; keep the referral system optional until purchase and support flows are proven
- [ ] Push to your pre-launch email/WhatsApp/Telegram list first (warm audience converts better and gives you early ratings before wider push)
- [ ] Post in relevant teacher-exam forums/communities (Quora CTET threads, relevant subreddits, Telegram groups you don't own) — informational tone, not salesy, pointing to genuinely free-tier value
- [ ] Ask your earliest users directly for Play Store ratings — early rating velocity matters a lot for Play Store algorithm visibility

## Phase 4: Ongoing Growth (Post-Launch)

- [ ] Keep the Telegram/WhatsApp daily-question habit going — retention engine + upsell funnel
- [ ] Turn audio masterclass snippets into 60-second "concept in 60 seconds" Reels/Shorts — ready-made content from material you're already producing for the app
- [ ] SEO: publish study-guide previews as blog posts on the website — they rank for CTET topic searches and funnel into installs, and cost nothing beyond writing time
- [ ] Gamify referrals with a monthly leaderboard and small prizes (free premium months)
- [ ] Time major promotional pushes 6–8 weeks before each CTET exam notification window — that's when search volume and urgency peak, so save some marketing spend/effort for those windows rather than spreading evenly

For Season 1, keep this phase deliberately small. Record customer questions, support issues, activation, repeat practice, and paid conversion. Use those findings to choose the next season's content, pricing, marketing channels, and feature investment.

## Low-Cost Promotion Playbook

### Priority order

1. **Personal network:** Share a direct, honest message with teachers, D.El.Ed/B.Ed candidates, and CTET aspirants who may genuinely benefit.
2. **Owned channels:** Use the EasyCTET website, Telegram, WhatsApp, Instagram, and YouTube Shorts. One useful question or explanation is better than frequent generic promotion.
3. **Relevant communities:** Share helpful answers in CTET groups only where promotion is allowed. Lead with a free question or explanation and disclose that EasyCTET is your product.
4. **Small creator partnerships:** Offer free access to a few relevant educators for an honest review or walkthrough. Avoid paid influencer campaigns during Season 1.
5. **Referral testing:** Add or promote referrals only after the purchase and support flows are stable.

### Sustainable weekly rhythm

- One daily question in Telegram or WhatsApp.
- One or two short concept posts or videos each week.
- One weekly message showing a real product improvement, learner insight, or study tip.
- One direct launch reminder during the final approach to the exam, without urgency spam.

### Spending rule

Spend first on the domain, Play Console, required legal and payment setup, reliable email, and essential app assets. Do not spend on ads, elaborate video production, premium social tools, or broad influencer campaigns until organic users demonstrate repeat use and willingness to pay.

### Promotion message pattern

Use this structure in every channel: **the Paper 1 problem, one useful insight, what EasyCTET provides, and one clear action.** Example: “Confusing conservation with centration? Here is the distinction. EasyCTET gives you 150 CDP questions with explanations. Start with the free Paper 1 practice.”

## Suggested Sequencing Note

Given the full-time job, the committed 6–8 daily hours, and the 9 October exam target, the schedule should be disciplined and founder-led:
- **Now to launch:** secure the essentials, finish a dependable and well-tested Paper 1 release, and seed one or two sustainable channels.
- **Launch window:** release through closed testing first, then invite a small warm audience before widening access.
- **Remainder of Season 1:** support customers, fix high-impact issues, and measure demand rather than adding broad new scope.
- **After the exam:** review evidence and plan the next season's larger content, marketing, and product investment.
