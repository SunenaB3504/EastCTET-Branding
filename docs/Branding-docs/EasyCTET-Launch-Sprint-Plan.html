Why November beats the exam-day spike

Moving the channel to November costs you the 9 October moment. It buys you three bigger ones.

The February 2026 exam ran on the 8th; its answer key appeared on 12 March and results on 30 March — about seven weeks. Applying the same lag to 9 October puts the answer key around mid-November and results around the end of November. The previous cycle's applications opened 17 November. All estimates — CBSE hasn't announced these — but the pattern is consistent.

02

One pipeline, three renderers

November and December now carry Hindi, Paper 2, the channel and the website simultaneously. That's only survivable if they're one piece of work wearing four hats.

You already have the question bank as structured JSON. The app reads it. Remotion can read the same file. So can a static site generator. Build it that way and the marginal cost of the third and fourth surfaces collapses to near zero. Build them as separate projects and this will genuinely be four times the work, and it won't happen.

Source

The question bank

One schema, versioned, with English and Hindi fields side by side, plus the explanation for every option. Get this right once — everything downstream is a thin renderer.

Renderer 1

The app

Already built. Paper 2 is new content in the same shape, not a new product.

Renderer 2 & 3

Remotion + static site

One React template renders a Short from a question. A second renders that same question as an indexed web page. Both consume the identical JSON.

Where the real risk sits

Code is clearly not your bottleneck — you built the app in five days. Content is. Paper 2 isn't one product either: candidates choose either the Mathematics & Science track or Social Studies, so it's two question banks. Across both papers, both tracks and both languages you're looking at roughly 3,000 question-and-explanation units.

Sequence it: Hindi for Paper 1 first, then Paper 2 CDP, then Paper 2 Maths & Science, then Social Studies. If the last one slips, ship without it and say so plainly rather than shipping it thin.

The quality trap

CTET Hindi-medium uses specific standardised pedagogy terminology, and approximately-right Hindi is worse than no Hindi — this audience spots it instantly and says so in reviews. A wrong answer key in a paid app undoes 150 good reviews. Spend October's revenue on a subject expert to check the Hindi if you can.

03

The YouTube channel

Remotion suits you specifically because your content is already data. Most CTET creators film themselves for twenty minutes per video; you'd render overnight.

Three pillars, each from something you already own

PillarFormatBuilt fromJob
Today's Trap Shorts, 30–45s The 750 questions you've already written Discovery — Shorts is the one surface YouTube still pushes hard to non-subscribers
Topic explainers 8–12 min The masterclass scripts, cut into single topics Search and authority — these still pull traffic in two years
Cycle events Reactive Answer key, results, memory-based questions Spikes — and Remotion lets you ship the same evening

Your differentiator maps unusually well onto Shorts. Most CTET Shorts just show a question and its answer. Yours explains why the wrong option was tempting — which is the hook, not the footnote.

The killer feature of choosing Remotion

Same component, two data sets, two videos. Bilingual output for roughly 10% extra effort — and Hindi-medium CTET YouTube is a larger audience than English. The Hindi content you're building for the app in November is the Hindi content for the channel. One investment, three surfaces.

Two production decisions that save real pain

  • Make the Shorts silent — text on screen, music bed. It sidesteps the Hindi text-to-speech quality problem completely, and it's the norm in this niche anyway. Voice is only needed for the explainers, where you already have recorded audio.
  • Render locally, overnight. Remotion Lambda costs money you'd rather spend on a Hindi subject expert. At your volume, batch rendering on your own machine is free and fast enough.

Retitle the explainers for search

"The Operating System of a Child's Mind" is a good episode title and a terrible video title. Nobody searches for it. Piaget's 4 Stages — CTET CDP is what people actually type. Keep the poetic titles inside the app; use search language on YouTube.

The faceless problem, and how to hold it

Indian exam-prep YouTube runs on trusted teacher-personalities, and an animated channel starts at a credibility disadvantage. Don't apologise for it — position it as the same promise the app makes: no ads, no login, no ten-minute intro before the content. Sirf questions, koi bakwaas nahi. That reads as a stance rather than a limitation.

04

The website's real job is search

Promotion is the smaller opportunity. The bigger one is that 750 questions are currently locked inside an app where Google cannot read them.

Aspirants search "ctet cdp piaget questions with answers" every day. If those questions exist as indexed pages on easyctet.in, you capture that traffic permanently — and unlike YouTube reach, SEO compounds rather than decays. You already generate video from that JSON; generating static pages is the same exercise.

  • Publish a slice of the question bank as pages — one per topic, question plus full explanation. Each page embeds its matching Short; each Short's description links back to the page.
  • Publish the masterclass scripts as articles. They're already written long-form in markdown. That's free SEO content with zero new writing.
  • Fix the dead-end CTA. The site's main call to action is currently a mailto: link, which converts badly in a mobile-first market. Replace it with the opt-in channel and a subscribe link.
  • Tag the Play links. Use utm_source=youtube and utm_source=website so Play Console tells you exactly what each surface converts — the same mechanism that runs the affiliate programme.
What this adds up to

The app monetises, the channel discovers, the website compounds. All three read the same JSON, and the only thing you actually have to keep producing is questions — which is the one thing you were going to produce anyway.
